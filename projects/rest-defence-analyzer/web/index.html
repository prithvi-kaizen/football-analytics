What these measurements mean

Definitions, outcomes and score

Behind-ball coverage counts outfield players level with or goal-side of the ball; the goalkeeper is separate. RMS compactness measures spread around their centroid. Central exposure uses a 2 m grid in a pitch-clipped 30 × 20 m rectangle toward the losing team's own goal, marking cells more than 5 m from every behind-ball outfield player.

Counter options are opposition outfield players at least 3 m toward the losing team's goal and within 40 m of the ball. Clear corridors have no losing-team outfield player within 2 m of the straight segment, excluding endpoints. Speed, orientation, pressure, offside and aerial flight are not modelled.

The score averages numerical shortfall, RMS spread, central exposure and clear-corridor fraction, using fixed declared constants. No model was fitted. Features use the last complete 5 Hz player snapshot strictly before the 25 Hz possession switch.

Outcomes stop at the first regain, inactive ball, gap, unknown control, half end or ten seconds. Incomplete observations are censored and excluded from outcome rates. Shots prefer available corrected frame references; other timestamps remain approximate.

Sources, reliability and reproducibility

One complete Düsseldorf–Nürnberg match is validated here: 293 accepted turnovers, including 168 in the default attacking-loss scope. All 293 behind-ball counts were independently checked against native XML. Seven selected source matches support method development and case studies, not league-wide claims.

This website filters a reviewed historical snapshot. It does not rerun the Python detector or analyze new matches. Replays use recorded 5 Hz player frames without interpolation; the ball-movement chart uses the native 25 Hz stream.
