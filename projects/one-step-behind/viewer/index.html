How a candidate is defined

Use the defending team's forward axis. Identify 3–5 metadata defenders, at least two centre backs, exactly the deepest N outfield players. Require ≥12 m width, ≤12 m spread and complete outfield geometry. Exclude ambiguous shapes.

A step starts from ≤3 m spread. At least N−1 players must move ≥1.5 m in a second, with ≥0.5 m in each half-second. A candidate needs a gap above tolerance, ≥1.5 m gap growth, peers within 3 m and no more than half their forward movement, sustained for 0.5 s.

Recovery requires the whole line within tolerance for 0.5 s. Missing data or context changes censor follow-up. No primary candidates means recovery distributions are unavailable.

Why this is not a tactical-error detector

A deeper defender may be covering a runner, protecting space or following an instruction absent from tracking. Metadata roles cannot establish a tactical line in every shape. Extrapolation can hide or create a difference. No independently labelled examples exist, so precision and accuracy are unknown.

The two baseline flags are shown because they challenge an instantaneous-gap rule. The highlighted player is inspected, not blamed. Threshold changes do not create ground truth.
